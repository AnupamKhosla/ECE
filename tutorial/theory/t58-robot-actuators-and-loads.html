Robot actuators, loads and drive limits

Start at the wheel or tool where work happens. Work backward through gearing, motor, drive and supply to find what can actually deliver that motion.

Time 40 minutesBefore this T03 · T19

Translate a task into force and speed

For a wheeled robot moving uphill, required longitudinal force is approximately F = ma + mg sin(α) + Crrmg cos(α). The terms describe acceleration, gravity along the slope and rolling resistance. A passive caster, surface transitions and load distribution can add effects worth measuring. With two equally loaded driven wheels, each supplies half the force in this example. Wheel torque is force per wheel × wheel radius. Wheel angular speed is linear speed divided by radius. Check available traction as well as motor torque. A slipping wheel can show high RPM with very little chassis progress.

ωmotor = Gωwheel   ·   τmotor ≈ τwheel / (Gη)

G is motor revolutions per wheel revolution. With 0.18 N·m at the wheel, 30:1 gearing and 75% assumed efficiency, the motor needs 0.008 N·m at the shaft. A 50 mm radius wheel at 0.50 m/s turns at 10 rad/s, so the same motor must supply that torque near 2,865 rpm. Check the speed and torque together on the motor’s curve and confirm the gearbox’s own output limit. Larger wheels trade force for speed at a given shaft torque and rpm.

Current delivers torque and creates heat

In a simple brushed DC motor model, V ≈ IR + L di/dt + keω, and electromagnetic torque is ktI. Back EMF rises with speed. At standstill the back EMF is zero, and steady current would approach V/R if the drive and supply allowed it. Driver current regulation can limit this current. Compare driver, motor and gearhead continuous ratings and peak limits, including the specified time and thermal conditions. Account for both motors starting together and for motor current versus source current under PWM. Driver and battery current limits apply at different measurement points.

During deceleration a moving load can feed energy back into the DC bus. Read the driver’s recirculation and braking documentation and check what the power source can absorb. A supply that can deliver current might still have no way to sink it. A vertically loaded axis can move after power loss unless the application has an appropriate means to hold the load.

A shorted motor can brake while dissipating energy in its winding and bridge switches. Regeneration routes some mechanical energy back to the source or the bus capacitor. An ideal capacitor accepting energy E would rise from V1 to √(V1² + 2E/C). Determine how much energy actually reaches that node and check its peak current and voltage. Drive choiceWhat to verify

StepperTorque at the demanded speed and acceleration. An open-loop count of commanded steps cannot reveal lost motion after a stall.
Servo drive with feedbackEncoder reference, following error, drive/brake behavior, and torque and speed limits under continuous and intermittent duty.

For a manipulator, add tool, adapter and part mass. Estimate gravity torque about the joint from the perpendicular moment arm. A 3 kg load at 0.30 m gives about 8.8 N·m from gravity alone when the lever is horizontal. Starting and stopping adds inertia-dependent torque, and the arm’s own mass adds load.
