P145

Commission a mobile robot fleet on a paper warehouse

An indoor robot can reach a goal and still fail the warehouse job. Track the payload, docking proof, shared space and charging all the way through a mission.

Time 60–90 minutesBefore this P141 · T59

Estimate capacity before drawing routes

A warehouse needs 18 loaded transfers per hour. A representative trip spends 35 s at pickup, 85 s travelling loaded, 40 s at drop-off and 80 s returning. One robot can make 15 ideal trips per hour. If charging, waiting and recovery leave 80% of the hour usable at that cycle rate, plan about 12 transfers per robot-hour. Two robots give about 24 per hour in this simple estimate, before additional shared-resource effects. Measure the station dwell times, loaded routes and charge profile to test the assumption.

Simulate two missions and a lost link

Draw two waiting bays, one at either end of aisle X. Only one vehicle can be authorised to enter X at once in this teaching model. Each traverse uses X for 80 s and each completed job needs two traverses. Robots A and B request at the same time. Assign A first and keep B’s request queued. At t = 35 s A goes offline and its last state is inside X. An authorised supervisor confirms it clear at t = 120 s. Record dispatch actions at 0, 35, 80 and 120 s.

Check the reservation and capacity conclusion

At 0 s reserve X to A. At 35 s hold the reservation and retain last-known occupancy. At 80 s the estimated clear time means little after the lost link. At 120 s release X after verified clearance and dispatch B if its request is still valid. Two 80 s traversals give an ideal aisle ceiling of 22.5 completed trips per hour. The estimated 24 trips per hour for two robots cannot exceed this ceiling, and recurring losses or queueing can put the actual rate below 18.

Try two aisles with conflicting requests. Make each robot reserve only the zone it can enter and provide a waiting location outside contested areas. Decide which vehicle yields if both are waiting for the next zone. Check whether a very large payload or a blocked holding bay invalidates your routing rule. These are scheduling policies for a paper exercise. Installed traffic and personnel protection require site assessment and qualified validation.
