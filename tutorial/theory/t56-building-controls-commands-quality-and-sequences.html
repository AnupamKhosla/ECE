T56

Building control sequences, command priorities and point quality

Understand schedules, permissions, feedback, alarms and timestamps independently of a vendor’s interface.

Time 25–40 minutesBefore this C01, T31, T34

Model the physical loop and where it closes

A schedule establishes when the equipment should serve a demand. A permission allows a sequence to proceed. A command asks equipment to act, and proof measures whether it did. The controller compares measured feedback to a setpoint and chooses an output within the limits of its sequence. Independent safeties can act outside that software loop. Name each point, give it units and quality, and draw the direction of control before looking at a graphic.

Tridium demonstrates Niagara views that expose parts of the model.

Understand whose command currently wins

For BACnet commandable properties, the effective value is the highest active priority, with 1 highest through 16 lowest. Relinquishing a command removes that entry by writing NULL at that priority. If every entry is NULL, the Relinquish_Default applies. A controller can separately enforce a safety veto or an equipment interlock. Confirm the physical result after releasing a training override. Follow the building’s approved authorization and priority conventions when reviewing a real site.

Keep alarms and trends tied to a point and clock

An alarm can be normal, active or awaiting acknowledgement depending on the system. Acknowledging communicates receipt; fixing the cause returns the condition to normal. A trend can sample periodically or capture changes, depending on system support. Include enough timing resolution to distinguish a fan starting from proof appearing. A value recorded as 21.2 °C without quality or timestamp can be either healthy and current or the last reading from a failed sensor.
