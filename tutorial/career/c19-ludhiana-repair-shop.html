Check for an actual service gap in two weeks

  1. Use the shop counter first. Review the last 30 days of jobs with the owner. Record requests turned away, repairs sent out and repeat brands. Ask customers who already use the counter which equipment they can drop off and what they currently send away. Check whether one repeat set of models can be tested on your bench. Ask delivering customers for the matching sample, charger, cable or consumable.

  2. Ask about their last breakdown. In Hindi or Punjabi, ask which machine stopped, its model and fault, where it went, how long it was out and what the business paid. Ask whether the person who paid would try a local bench diagnostic. Get a model plate photo or part number with permission.

  3. Check who can do the same job. Compare an authorised route, a nearby specialist and a dealer who sends equipment out. Ask who repairs in-house, how they demonstrate the result, what the diagnosis costs and what the written warranty covers. Log the date and replies. Hypertherm repair locator · GeM public purchase searches.

  4. Run one narrow pilot. Find three recent cases from separate businesses with a common fault or model, plus a donor or suitable fixture. Then quote one paid diagnostic with a test and time limit. Those counts are proposed starting criteria for the trial. Record jobs that failed the test and cases customers solved by replacing the unit.

Work a hypothetical job. If a ₹1,200 invoice uses ₹300 in parts, ₹150 in transport and ₹100 reserved for warranty work, ₹650 remains before labour, tax and fixed overhead. At 1½ hours of your time that is about ₹433 per hour before labour and overhead. Replace the example figures with your records when you price the work.

Ask for repeat work before ordering spares, and add only models you can reliably identify and test. Use separate lines for diagnosis, parts and visits on the quote. C16 repair workflow and handover · Printable job sheet · Public research notes.
