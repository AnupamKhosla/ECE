One terminology check while you watch: ST classifies the SG3525 as a voltage-mode PWM controller. A circuit using it can regulate welding current through external feedback. Seeing a current transformer does not by itself establish that the controller is a peak-current-mode PWM IC. See ST’s SG3525 documentation and T21.

Read a service manual as a diagnostic textbook

A useful example is Lincoln Electric SVM177-A, V160-S & V160-T, for the specific machine code numbers listed on its cover. The manufacturer-authored manual includes theory of operation, test procedures, diagrams and retest guidance. The available link below is a third-party mirror of that manufacturer document. It teaches the structure of professional service work; its test values and discharge procedure must not be transplanted to a generic MMA-200.

Lincoln SVM177-A · manufacturer service manual, hosted by ManualsLibLincoln IM737 · V160-S operator’s manual on the manufacturer’s server

  1. Check the cover and code numbers. Tell an operator’s manual from a service manual. Identify which variant and revision the diagrams cover.

  2. Read theory of operation. Draw the power path and auxiliary/control paths. Compare them with W01’s representative diagram.

  3. Choose one symptom. Follow its troubleshooting branch and list every expected observation. Explain why each measurement eliminates a candidate cause.

  4. Read retest after repair. List what would be missed by replacing the visibly damaged part and trying one arc.

Use this worksheet for every tutorial

  1. Machine: exact model, board revision, topology and source of the schematic.
  2. Complaint: the customer’s symptom, separated from the presenter’s diagnosis.
  3. Prediction: expected meter mode, probe polarity, reference and reading before the test.
  4. Observation: what was actually measured; was the part in circuit or isolated?
  5. Explanation: physics lesson, datasheet condition and competing causes.
  6. Closure: why the part failed and which post-repair tests were completed or omitted.

If a video swaps a part without a measurement or never returns to load/thermal testing, record those missing steps. You can still learn component recognition from it without treating it as a complete diagnostic procedure.

A concrete three-session assignment

  1. Session 1: complete W02. Submit a drawing, both current paths and your eight bridge readings. Explain one deliberately open diode.

  2. Session 2: watch the block-level welder demonstration and redraw its energy path. Open a datasheet for one bridge, one output diode and one switching device; identify the terminals and rating conditions.

  3. Session 3: work through one Lincoln symptom and one W05 case. Produce a fault tree and fill R04 using only known facts, marking the rest “not measured”. Discuss it with your current repair technician.

Skills checklist

  • I can use a video without copying unsupported assumptions.
  • I can match a service manual to exact machine codes.
  • I have read rating conditions in a bridge, rectifier and transistor datasheet.
  • I have completed a written diagnosis with its uncertainty recorded.
