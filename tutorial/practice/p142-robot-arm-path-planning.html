P142

Plan an arm path around a fixture

Two poses can be reachable while the path between them strikes a fixture. Check the whole arm along the move, then decide how long a valid move would take.

Time 65–90 minutesBefore this P43 · T33

Separate pose, path and trajectory

A pose describes position and orientation. The trainer controls only x and y because this two-joint arm has no independent tool orientation. A geometric path connects positions. A trajectory tells the controller where to be at each time and must respect joint velocity and acceleration limits. Joint interpolation can make a curved tool path. A straight tool path usually takes nonlinear joint motion. Keep both views when checking clearance.

Test a direct route and a detour

Start at (180, −75) mm and finish at (180, 75) mm. The links are 150 mm and 100 mm. The fixture’s closed rectangle spans x = 170–195 mm, y = −20–20 mm. The detour goes through (145, −75) and (145, 75) mm. The trainer samples straight tool segments every 5 mm or less. The joint-move option interpolates joint angles with steps of at most 2° per joint. Both modes check the link centre lines against the fixture and flag a singularity at full extension or fold. Real planning also checks joint limits, self-collision, attached workpieces, tooling, continuous clearance and controller-specific blending.

Check a route to see the first rejected pose or the final pose.

The dashed line shows the planned tool path. The rectangle marks the fixture. The solid lines show the links at the reported pose or the last reachable pose.

  1. Calculate both joint solutions at the start using T33. Change the branch in the same route and compare the first collision positions. The tool occupies the same target coordinate while the elbow moves.
  2. Compare the straight tool move with the direct joint move. They share endpoints. The joint move holds q₂ steady while q₁ turns, sweeping a circular arc where x reaches about 195 mm. The tool path with Cartesian interpolation stays at x = 180 mm. Compare where each first contacts the fixture.
  3. Compare direct and detour. Verify the 260 mm target is outside the arm’s reach. The full extension route reaches 250 mm, where the two links align and the arm loses one instantaneous direction of planar motion.
  4. After the ideal detour clears, give the gripper and part 15 mm outward extent in each direction and check clearance again. Inflating the rectangle by 15 mm in the teaching model is a conservative test if that envelope applies to the whole arm.

Time an axis move with acceleration limits

For one 60° joint move from rest to rest at 90°/s and 180°/s², acceleration and deceleration each take 0.50 s and cover 22.5°. The remaining 15° at 90°/s takes 0.167 s. Total time is about 1.17 s. A 30° move with the same limits has a triangular profile because there is insufficient distance to reach 90°/s. A coordinated move must fit all participating axes and the path. Comparing individual axis times gives a lower bound on coordinated duration.

Find the time for the 30° joint move

Each half covers 15°. From rest, 15° = ½ × 180°/s² × t², giving 0.408 s per half and 0.816 s in total. Peak speed is about 73.5°/s. This calculation ignores jerk limits and assumes equal acceleration and deceleration.
