P147

Prove a gripper can pick, keep and release a part

A close command, contact indication and retained part tell you three different things. Choose a gripper for the load, then prove the part stays with the tool until release.

Time 65–90 minutesBefore this P56 · T58

Follow the load through the tool

Watch. Trace the valve and the two actuator ports. On the vacuum trainer, trace supply, valve and cup separately before deciding where to measure. Bob Teaches Tech · 5/2 valve, double-acting cylinder and limit valves.

Watch · Identify where the jaws contact the part and which motion introduces a side load. Universal Robots · Machine tending gripper demonstration.

For a vertical friction grip, the available upward force from two identical opposing jaws is approximately 2μN, where N is the normal force per jaw. A 0.8 kg test part accelerating upward at 2 m/s² requires 9.45 N upward. With an assumed margin factor of 2 and measured friction coefficient μ = 0.30, each jaw needs about 31.5 N. Check whether a vendor quotes force per jaw or total force. A longer finger can reduce force or exceed a moment limit. Workpiece damage, oil and jaw alignment matter too.

For vacuum, theoretical normal force is pressure difference × effective sealed area. Two cups each providing 300 mm² at 50 kPa below ambient give 30 N combined normal force in the ideal calculation. If one cup has no seal while the other still achieves the stated pressure, the estimate falls to 15 N and misses the 18.9 N training requirement. If they share a manifold, the leak can reduce vacuum at both cups. A vertical surface introduces a shear limit, and an off-centre load introduces a peeling moment. Use the vendor’s rated conditions and data measured on representative parts for a real selection. Schmalz · Holding-force examples for different load directions.

Reconstruct four fault records

In this recorded trainer, vacuum is shown as a positive magnitude below ambient. The nominal recipe requires at least 45 kPa within 300 ms of commanding vacuum, 100 ms continuously above that threshold before transfer, and continued proof during transfer. Each case starts at t = 0. Real process thresholds depend on the tooling and part. Use the last observed proof together with independent part presence.

Recreate a sealing fault by hand

Press a small hand-operated suction cup onto a smooth tile on the bench. With the tile kept flat and supported, compare seal and release with the same cup on a porous or grooved sample lying flat. Repeat each three times and record surface, condition and result. Mark the point in the real sequence where your difference would first appear.
