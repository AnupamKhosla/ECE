Welding Repair Track · W06

Parts, first power-up and return to service

Turn a confirmed diagnosis into a durable repair: choose compatible parts, restore insulation and cooling, then document model-specific electrical and load checks.

Study + exercises 90 min + mentored workshop workLevel After W03–W05Context India · family repair shop

Decide whether the job is repairable

Make the repair decision before ordering parts. Add component cost, labour, diagnostic uncertainty and the required retest to the estimate. A charred isolation barrier, badly damaged board, unavailable critical part or missing service information may make a board exchange or referral more sensible than component replacement. Record the reason and tell the customer what the estimate covers.

Use the full model/code/board revision, not the front-panel amp number. Keep removed parts labelled with the job number until handover. Parts from traceable distributors reduce uncertainty; appearance and printed markings alone do not authenticate a semiconductor.

Replacement specification checklist

PartMatch or evaluateCommon mistake
Input bridgePin arrangement, VRRM, average/RMS current conditions, surge/I²t, thermal path and mounting isolation.Reading the 25 A or 35 A headline without its case-temperature and cooling conditions.

A faster switch can create more overshoot; a higher-voltage transistor may have more loss; a larger gate charge may overburden the driver. Manufacturer-approved replacements are preferred. If the design requires a group/module replacement, follow it. There is no universal rule that every parallel device must always be replaced, or that replacing just one is always acceptable.

Restore the assembly as an electrical system

  1. Repair the failed circuit and its cause. Recheck related components before fitting an expensive power device. Carbonised PCB material and damaged isolation distances need a manufacturer-appropriate repair or board replacement.

  2. Restore insulation and cooling. Refit the correct pads, sleeves, earth bonds, barriers, clamps and cable routes. A metal transistor tab may be electrically live. Follow the specified mounting torque and interface materials.

  3. Inspect workmanship. Verify polarity and orientation, inspect for solder bridges and debris, compare connectors with photographs and perform the service manual’s cold checks. Log unresolved items.

Plan the first power-up with your mentor

There is no universal “put a bulb in series and switch it on” sequence for welding inverters. PFC, undervoltage protection, precharge relays and auxiliary supplies can respond differently to limited input power. Reduced input voltage can also prevent the expected control sequence. The correct sequence and current-limiting arrangement must come from the model’s service procedure and the technician’s equipped bench.

Before energising, the technician chooses expected rail values, waveform checks, acceptable current and explicit stop conditions. Probes and barriers are arranged in the de-energised state. An unexpected current rise, abnormal sound, heating, missing drive or unstable rail means stop and investigate—not repeated fuse replacement. Every return inside after power-up requires isolation and stored-energy verification again.

Protection is part of the repair

Do not defeat thermal sensors, overcurrent paths, interlocks or protective earth to force operation. Do not float a normal grounded scope. For the instrument-reference issue, revisit W01.

What closes a repair

Check categoryRecord actual result and procedure
Protective measuresCord/plug and earth-bond condition, protective-conductor test for applicable Class I equipment, and model-appropriate insulation/leakage or other safety tests. Apply the manufacturer’s test points, limits and method.
Control operationCorrect mode, current setting response, relevant fan/sensor operation, VRD/indicators/protection according to the service instructions.
Output performanceSpecified open-circuit behaviour and controlled loaded voltage/current at stated settings using rated equipment; account for meter bandwidth and pulsed waveforms.
Thermal / duty-cycle behaviourPerformance at defined current, ambient, duration and duty cycle with correct airflow. A brief arc is not a thermal endurance test.
Final assembly / handoverAll barriers and covers restored, labels/serial retained, lead condition recorded, limits explained, checks signed and dated by the responsible technician.

Do not apply a generic insulation-tester voltage indiscriminately across connected electronic boards, surge devices or welding terminals. The model’s procedure determines what is connected, which instrument and test voltage to use, and the acceptance limits. A continuity beep and “it welds” do not substitute for these checks.

Use a suitable service load arrangement rather than improvised shorts or a handheld meter on its amp range. Welding trials also involve arc radiation, fumes, hot metal and fire precautions; use a competent operator and the required welding setup.

Build the service in stages at your shop

Start by improving intake, lead assessment and records while the existing technician still performs repairs. Then shadow cold diagnosis, complete donor-board work and take on a narrow, familiar machine family under supervision. Keep unfamiliar AC/DC TIG, three-phase, engine-driven and badly damaged equipment on a referral route until their requirements are understood.

For every completed job, log total time, parts, repair outcome and any return. Use that record to decide which repairs your shop can support reliably. Offer a clear scope and handover record based on completed tests.

The repair produces an arc for 20 seconds. Is it ready for the customer?

No. That shows limited function for a short interval. Complete the applicable protective measures, loaded performance, control, thermal/duty-cycle and final-assembly checks using the matching service procedure, then record the results.
