P137

Learn robotics from the first motor to a production cell

Build a robot by proving one link at a time. An encoder count must become the right distance before a map can mean anything. A completed job must correspond to a physical part before a production counter can be trusted.

Time 20 minutes to planBefore this P00 · ECE foundation

Start with one input and one output

A robot combines a mechanism, a power source, actuators, sensors and a controller. Take a two-wheel line follower. The battery feeds a motor driver. Two reflectance sensors measure the floor. A controller estimates which side of the line it is on and changes wheel duty. Wheels move the frame, so the sensors see a new part of the floor. An encoder adds a separate measurement of wheel rotation. The same boundaries appear on a factory arm, where the joints, tooling and fixture each contribute to where the part ends up.

Build your first bench robot

  1. Use P02 and P03 to make and measure a circuit. Use P11 to prove an input and output in firmware. Check that the output returns to its starting state after a reset.
  2. Read a reflectance signal over dark and light material as in P12. Measure the gap to the paper and keep it fixed during the comparison. Set the threshold only after you know the two readings.
  3. Use P13 to identify the motor, driver and separate logic and power connections. Use the hardware route in P28 with a small extra-low-voltage chassis, or practise the motor and motion calculations in the browser. The browser exercises run locally and need no robot.
  4. Raise and secure the chassis for P28’s first direction test. Then verify encoder sign and scale for each wheel in P42. Record command, wheel movement and counts for the same test.
