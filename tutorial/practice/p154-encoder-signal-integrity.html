P154

Find missed and extra encoder pulses at the receiver

A counter can disagree with a marked turn because the wire distorted the pulse, the input saw two edges, or the software counted a different kind of edge. Prove each boundary.

Time 55–80 minutesBefore this P15 · P42 · T40

Start with a repeatable pattern

Ask what the instrument counts. One rising edge of channel A, both edges of A and all valid quadrature transitions produce different numbers per revolution. Use a known number of pulses or a marked turn, then record source waveform, receiver waveform, accepted transitions and the configured count mode. On a two-channel encoder, also check that nearby transitions retain their expected order.

Measure a slow rising edge on an isolated 5 V bench

With the Uno powered off, put a 4.7 kΩ resistor from D7 to its 5 V rail. There is no motor or field wiring in this exercise. Power the Uno by USB. Tie the scope reference to the trainer ground and probe D7 with an appropriately configured 10× probe. D7 alternates between driving low and going high impedance. The external pull-up creates the rising edge.

void setup() {
  digitalWrite(7, LOW);
}

void loop() {
  pinMode(7, OUTPUT);
  delayMicroseconds(40);
  pinMode(7, INPUT);
  delayMicroseconds(40);
}
  1. Capture the rise with no external capacitor. Add a 150 pF ceramic from D7 to ground with power off, then repeat. Compare again with 680 pF. Keep the same probe, reference and time setting. The capacitor represents the load a signal might see on a long cable.
  2. Measure time to 70% of the final voltage as a modelling exercise. With 680 pF and 4.7 kΩ, predict about 3.85 µs ignoring probe and stray capacitance. The receiver’s real threshold and edge-rate limits come from its data sheet. Compare your measurement with the model.
  3. Switch the scope to a logic view if available. Compare threshold-based transitions with the analogue waveform. Explain why a logic trace can hide the low high-level margin in a short pulse. Use the recorded fault table for short pulses and load-switching evidence.

A longer software debounce can suppress a fast burst and still drop legitimate pulses at top speed. Before changing it, compare the shortest valid interval with the signal and receiver specifications. Keep one trace before the change and one after at the same pulse rate.
