P73

Triage an outage in a network operations centre

Use three reachability checks and a change record to work out who is affected. Write the incident handover the next shift will need.

Desk and lab35–55 minAfter P71

What the operator does

A network operations centre (NOC) groups alerts, checks the impact, contacts the responsible team and keeps the incident record current. A field technician can inspect, test or reseat a specifically authorised asset. An operator can correlate the time and symptoms across services before dispatch. Learn the distinction between management reachability, link state and service reachability.

Watch a real Network Operations Centre. Listen for the difference between observing an outage and sending someone to the affected network. Telstra Enterprise on YouTube.

Make a monitoring bench

Use an owned router with two lab computers and an Ethernet switch. Connect one computer directly to a router LAN port as your monitoring station. Connect the other to a downstream switch. Write down the gateway and downstream host IP addresses from their own network settings. Disable alternative paths such as Wi-Fi on the downstream test computer. An optional third computer or Raspberry Pi behind the same switch gives you another affected endpoint. Use the case below on paper if you have a single computer.

  1. At the monitoring station, use ping to check the gateway and downstream hosts. Record their addresses as labels G, A and B in the portfolio. Check that each destination actually answers ICMP before starting. If a computer blocks ping, run a known lab service on it and test that service consistently.
  2. Unplug the downstream switch uplink on the isolated bench. Repeat the checks. Look at the link indicators on both ends. Restore the cable to its original port and record the first successful checks and their times.
  3. Repeat with one endpoint's cable. Compare the different scope. Check whether the router remains reachable in both experiments and whether only the endpoint or the whole branch failed.

Work the shift case

All times and device labels are synthetic. The diagram in the work order is monitor → G → aggregate switch → uplink U7 → downstream switch → services A and B. The upstream switch management address is reached from G without using U7.

09:01A and B respond normally. G and the upstream switch management port respond.Last known good time for this monitoring interval.
09:02A and B time out. G and the upstream switch management IP still respond. The upstream switch reports U7 link down.Match U7 with the circuit record. Check whether other branches work.
09:04Other branches work. A planned change is listed for U9.Ask the change owner what was physically touched. Log the overlap as a possibility.
09:11Remote hands restores U7, reports a disconnected patch lead and reads back its labels. Link and service probes recover.Repeat both application checks, capture the restored link state and confirm the declared impact.

You can locate the affected branch by 09:02. The precise cause needs the later physical observation. A successful gateway ping says the management station can reach that gateway. It cannot confirm that either downstream service works.

Write a handover someone can act on

Include timezone, first failure and last success, services and sites affected, a working comparison, upstream interface, evidence, current owner and next update time. Follow the employer's severity matrix and change authority. Keep personal details and infrastructure identifiers out of the public portfolio.

Submit your evidence

Save your link-state and probe matrix for normal operation, single endpoint failure and switch uplink failure. Add one operator note at 09:04 and a separate closure note after the application probes pass. Include what the receiving team would need to verify on site.
