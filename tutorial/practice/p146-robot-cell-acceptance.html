P146

Accept, hand over and troubleshoot a commercial robot cell

Turn a supplier demonstration into a decision you can stand behind. Follow one simulated machine-tending project from requirements to production handover and the first service call.

Time 90–120 minutesBefore this P142 · P144 · T60

Define the task before selecting a robot

The customer needs to load a CNC machine with two families of parts. The larger wrist assembly consists of a 1.8 kg gripper, a 0.4 kg adapter and a 1.2 kg workpiece. Their centres along the flange axis are 70, 25 and 180 mm from the flange. The combined payload is 3.4 kg, at about (1.8×70 + 0.4×25 + 1.2×180) ÷ 3.4 ≈ 104 mm in this simplified geometry. The empty tool is 2.2 kg at about 62 mm. Check both conditions against the manufacturer’s load, inertia and offset limits.

Choose a candidate layout. A six-axis arm can orient the workpiece to pass the CNC door and insert into a chuck. A gantry may be viable with fixed vertical access. Check every approach and retreat with the door open, actual tooling in the model and the worst part envelope. Compare grip proof, chuck and door feedback, part ID, access for service and a recovery path after an interrupted transfer.

Decide from the trial instead of the headline rate

In one synthetic eight-hour shift, 30 min are scheduled outside production. Of 450 planned production minutes, 390 are running. The cell counts 2,500 parts of which 2,425 are good. An ideal cycle reference is 8 s per part. The measured running-cycle P155 is 9.8 s using the agreed definition.

  1. Compute availability (run ÷ planned), performance (ideal cycle × total count ÷ run time, with time units matched), quality (good ÷ total) and their product, overall equipment effectiveness (OEE).
  2. Compute good units per planned production hour. Decide whether to accept the rate requirement now. Compare the median and P155 only if you have the cycle records. Use timestamped waits to find where the upper tail comes from.
  3. Estimate simple payback with a fictional cost of $96,000 installed, annual savings of $60,000 and $12,000 in added annual operating costs. Then reduce expected savings by 25%. Include integration, training, recurring support, spares and changeover when requesting a real quote.
Check the arithmetic and release decision

Availability ≈ 86.7%, performance ≈ 85.5%, quality = 97.0%, OEE ≈ 71.9%. Throughput ≈ 323.3 good units per planned hour, so throughput passes. P155 misses the 9.5 s limit by 0.3 s, so the combined rate requirement stays open. Base simple payback is 2.0 years. With savings down 25%, the annual net falls to $33,000 and payback is about 2.91 years.

Trace the slowest cycles in this separate 20-cycle diagnostic sample. All event timestamps use one clock. Compute cycle time as end minus start, station wait as ready minus start, and robot motion as motion end minus ready. Sort the cycle times and use the nearest-rank 95th percentile, the value at rank ceil(0.95 × N) counted from the shortest. Keep IDs so you can return to the corresponding events. This small synthetic sample demonstrates the method and the full-shift trial above sets the acceptance context.

Which interval accounts for the slowest cycles?

Rank 19 is 9.8 s, cycle 8. The maximum is 10.5 s, cycle 16. Station-ready wait rises to 1.80 s and 2.45 s. Robot motion lasts 4.54 s and 4.58 s, within the range of other cycles. Trace the CNC station-ready signal and reasons for waiting before changing robot speed.

Recover a fault and preserve the commissioned setup

After a gripper change, the arm reaches the same error repeatedly and the error scales with wrist orientation. Compare tool ID, active TCP, payload data and the parts record against the saved baseline. Confirm which change occurred before recalibration. Retest the same reference and at least one different orientation. If errors appear only at high speed with load, inspect the timestamped following-error and cycle records before changing calibration. Apply site procedures for all actual equipment work.

Hand over the approved drawings, I/O map, backed-up robot and PLC configuration, tool and calibration files, software revisions, agreed spares, fault recovery instructions, test results, open issues with responsible people and deadlines, and a documented restore test. Record operator and maintenance training against the actual tasks. Include a servo diagnosis trace, a CNC restart exercise and the retained part record and interruption results.
