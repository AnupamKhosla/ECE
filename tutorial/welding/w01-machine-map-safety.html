Welding Repair Track · W01

Identify the machine, map the stages, prepare the bench

Recognise the power-source family, draw its energy path and understand the workshop conditions needed before a real machine is opened.

Study + exercises 75–90 minLevel After W00 and P03Context India · family repair shop

Identify it from the outside first

Photograph the nameplate, controls, connectors and any damage. Record the exact model, serial/code number, rated input, output type and duty-cycle table. “MMA-200” is a broad marketing label used on different designs; it is not a schematic identifier. Weight and size are clues, not proof.

A representative inverter power path

With a simple bridge-and-capacitor input on nominal 230 V RMS, the unloaded bus is near 230 × √2 ≈ 325 V DC, minus diode drops. This is a calculation, not a live measurement task. PFC designs may regulate the bus higher; doubler, split-bus and three-phase designs differ. Do not use 325 V as a universal acceptance limit.

The output is not a fixed “12 V supply”. Open-circuit voltage, welding voltage, current regulation, VRD and protection behaviour depend on mode and model. A lit display or spinning fan proves only that some supply path works.

Stored energy and measurement references

E = ½ C V²
For 680 µF at 325 V: E ≈ 35.9 JVoltage determines both shock exposure and stored energy. Capacitors can retain charge, bleeders can fail, and voltage can rebound after discharge. A fixed wait time or an extinguished LED does not verify the capacitor bank.

Use the exact service manual’s discharge and verification procedure under supervision. Each capacitor in a series bank and relevant nodes to chassis may need separate verification. Never short a capacitor with a screwdriver, and never assume a small control board is isolated because its rail is labelled 15 V.

A normal bench oscilloscope’s probe ground is usually connected to protective earth; USB instruments may acquire an earth path through the computer. Clipping that ground to primary DC negative or a switching node can make a destructive short. Two ordinary probes with maths subtraction do not create isolation. A battery-powered scope is suitable only if its inputs and insulation are rated for the actual common-mode voltage and transients.

The measurement setup is part of the circuit

Never remove the scope’s protective earth or power a normal earthed scope through an isolation transformer to float it. A Variac does not provide isolation; a series lamp limits current but does not isolate or establish safe power-up conditions. A rated differential probe or appropriately isolated instrument requires a trained operator and correct voltage, category and common-mode ratings. See Tektronix’s floating-measurement guidance.

Buy tools in stages

  • First: low-voltage learning kit.Reliable diode-mode multimeter, protected leads, breadboard, resistors, 1N4007 diodes, small bridge, loose known-good MOSFET, 9 V battery and current-limited 5–12 V supply. Learn soldering on scrap low-voltage boards.
  • Next: cold repair work with a mentor.Model manuals, insulated hand tools, magnification, temperature-controlled soldering, suitable extraction and a capacitance/ESR meter. Inspect donor boards only after verified discharge. Use an ESD bench for loose devices; remove body-connected ESD straps for hazardous-voltage work.
  • Later: equipped service bench.Let the mentor specify the instruments, differential/isolated probing, safety-test equipment and load test arrangement required by the actual machines. A cheap “100 MHz scope” alone does not make a welding repair bench.

Keep the welding work-return clamp separate in your mind from the machine’s protective earth: the work lead carries welding current. It is not a replacement for the chassis earth conductor.

Map one machine without powering it

  1. From an exact manual, mark input, bridge, capacitor bank, switches, transformer, output rectifier and control board. Draw arrows for the energy path.

  2. Mark which regions are mains-referenced, which are isolated, and where the isolation components sit. Mark any uncertainty instead of assuming a “ground” symbol means protective earth.

  3. Compare with photographs taken by your technician after safe access. Write component references and full markings beside your map.

A board runs on 15 V. Can a grounded USB scope be attached?

Not on that fact alone. A 15 V rail can be referenced to the mains-connected primary bus. Determine the isolation boundary and the instrument’s permitted reference before any connection.

Skills checklist

  • I can identify the machine family from evidence.
  • I can draw the two rectifier stages and the isolation boundary.
  • I can explain why unplugging and waiting do not verify de-energisation.
  • I know which activities require my repair mentor and the exact service manual.
