P46

Check depth accuracy before a robot uses it

A depth camera says a tray is 750 mm away. Check the geometry, units and missing depth before a controller uses that number for movement.

Time 65 minutesBefore this P43 · P45 · T36
What you’ll use

Ruler or tape, textured cardboard and a plain or shiny sample. The browser exercise uses sample stereo measurements. An actual USB stereo camera with its viewer is optional. Keep the camera stationary and use stationary objects.

Identify the two views and coordinate frame

Two horizontally separated images see the same detail at different pixel positions. After rectification, matched features lie on the same horizontal image row. Their horizontal separation is disparity. A textured target is easier to match than a plain wall. Know which camera frame your depth is relative to.

Z = f × B ÷ df is the rectified focal length in pixels, B is the camera baseline, and d is disparity in pixels. Z is in the same unit as B.

Measure near and far targets

For the training camera use a 75 mm baseline and f = 600 px. Print textured and plain panels. If you have a camera, put the textured panel at three fixed distances and compare the viewer’s median valid depth over a small central region with a ruler referenced to the camera as specified by its maker.

Test the plain and shiny panels. Mark missing or low-confidence regions as invalid. Measure the percentage of valid pixels as well as the median of those that remain. Raw zero or a device-specific sentinel can mean invalid depending on the API.

Watch on YouTube · Look for what happens to depth when the relative camera geometry moves or is corrected. Dynamic Calibration: Keeping Stereo Depth Accurate · Luxonis.

Separate calibration, units and poor matches

A consistent ×1000 error is a unit mismatch candidate. A distance-dependent error can come from calibration or disparity. A hole that follows a shiny surface points toward matching or sensing conditions. If all targets are shifted after camera movement, check the camera-to-base transform. For your camera, save intrinsics, baseline, depth units, stream resolution and the data-quality setting.

Follow the official rectification/model references and your camera’s viewer documentation. OpenCV calibration and stereo models · Luxonis depth documentation.

Set up a defensible depth gate

For the worked example, mark a result unusable if fewer than 80% of the region’s pixels are valid, the sample is stale or the depth error exceeds a ground-truth check you choose. Log the cause and the test distances. Produce a software-only result before associating the measurement with a physical motion controller.
