ECE Workbench

Build a foundation in electronics by working at the bench. Then use what you can measure and explain to repair equipment, commission building controls or build your own systems.

Open P00 · Your ECE foundation and learning routes for your first three bench sessions and four skill checkpoints. Begin with P01 · Set up your bench, P02 · Build your first circuit and P03 · Choose a meter and make your first reading. Keep T00 · Maths toolkit nearby for units and formulas.

Make equipment in a small Indian workshop

  • Make a DC stick welding set · Design a 230 V inverter, fabricate the case and assemble the first unit. Make and test the welding leads, then qualify and price the pilot batch.
  • Build workshop blowers. Start with a 12 V bench prototype, then build a battery-powered version or a direct 230 V corded version. Measure installed airflow on the finished build, or make and align shaft supports to learn how the rotor fits.
  • Work on the motor. Inspect a corded blower motor and wind a practice field coil with a former you make by hand.

Study a complete route

  • Electronics foundations. Start with simple breadboard builds and follow one of those circuits onto copper.
  • BMS and building controls. Start at the field device, run an air-handler simulation and follow the same evidence into Niagara, Siemens and other platforms.
  • Laptop and peripheral repair. Diagnose Windows, Linux, Mac and ChromeOS machines. Follow service cases for Lenovo Yoga Book, Dell Latitude, Samsung Galaxy Book, iClever keyboards and mice.

Pick a practical skill to develop

Choose an outcome below. The full roadmap gives the exercises in order and the evidence to keep.

Find out why equipment fails

  • Bench diagnosis · Locate a weak connection under load, follow an audio signal to the speaker and keep the before-and-after readings.
  • Laptop service · Distinguish power, boot and peripheral complaints. Collect evidence from display, cooling and input tests before deciding whether to open the case.
  • Android repair · Test touch, sound and charging before removing a module. Compare lens, software and module faults in the camera lab.
  • Welding equipment · Learn the repair sequence and build the current-control trainer for a DC stick set.

Install and commission field equipment

  • Building controls and site work · Fit and label a sensor on a mock wall, then prove the room, cable, terminal and screen point agree.
  • Industrial automation · Prove start, stop and independent feedback. Trace a fault from SCADA trend to drive, motor or pneumatic actuator.
  • Security and communications · Prove a camera has power, link and usable playback. Learn copper testing, fibre loss and antenna reception as separate jobs.

Build a system and prove it is reliable

  • Raspberry Pi and embedded systems · Prove boot, link and service on a Pi. Debug a Pico program or trace one IoT reading from sensor to dashboard.
  • Robotics from bench to production · Build your first robot, then choose mobile navigation, arms and vision or factory commissioning.
  • Machine vision · See if a decision survives reseating a part. Then test depth and AI on unseen parts before using either in a robot cell.
  • Board design, instrumentation and energy · Prove a connector fits, build a board test fixture, verify a sensor calibration or log a device’s energy use.

Test used laptop parts before you put them back into service

For a box of old machines or loose parts, start by sorting and pricing the donor lot. Set up a reference laptop so each test can establish which part failed.

  • Memory. Check the module in known-good slots, then inspect a suspect board cold. Learn why a bad part of a rank is hard to isolate.
  • Storage. Check SATA or NVMe drive health while keeping the data-handling decision separate. See how an SSD retires bad blocks.
  • Cooling. Match a donor fan and heatsink, then use the same load to compare airflow and temperature before and after.
  • Displays. Separate a faulty panel from its cable, then prove the donor at boot and across the full image.
  • Charging jacks. Test a removable DC-in harness with a known-good adapter and an exact replacement.

Diagnose laptop boards and LCD televisions

  • Laptop motherboard. Trace input, standby and run rails, then localise a short to one branch. Continue to prove and replace an open board fuse. Use the ThinkPad T480 board example to compare, source and verify a replacement.
  • LCD television. Compare a built-in picture and the backlight. Learn what the boards, glass and LED driver do and practise a strip fault and panel handling. For a lit but distorted picture, isolate the image path and qualify a timing board.

Learn the equipment used in commercial controls

  • Building controls. Follow filter pressure tubes, check a water valve and actuator, prove fan airflow and compare a CO₂ sensor with a room reference. Then verify VAV airflow at two setpoints.
  • Electrical and machine service. Read a power meter and CT ratio, check a replacement smart sensor and trace a shared 24 V dropout. Start with the photoelectric counting lab to learn what makes an optical sensor switch.

Train for telecom work in Melbourne

Start with the Melbourne telecom job guide to compare desk and field work, read employer examples and choose a case you can show at an interview.

  • Internet and mobile support. Practise locating a broadband fault and separating a coverage fault from an activation issue.
  • Data centres and field work. Trace both ends of a fibre work order or check a coax lead and signal report.
  • Network operations and voice. Scope a shared outage and diagnose one-way audio. Look up the service layers in T44.

Offer drop-off repairs in Ludhiana

Use the Ludhiana repair-shop guide to choose equipment you can take at the counter, test on your own bench and hand back with a recorded result.

  • Fabrication. Add plasma-cutter triage to welding repair. Then service a torch and compare gas delivery under flow. Learn to verify coolant flow for equipment that uses an external chiller.
  • Packaging and cold storage. Diagnose a weak pouch seal, a label feed or print defect or a cold-room temperature alarm. Work through a Zebra ZD421D platen service and print comparison.
  • Machine sensor wiring. Prove a dropout in a detached sensor cable before quoting a sensor, connector or controller. If the fault follows the termination, fit and prove a field-wireable M12 end.

Bench practice and real equipment

Beginner bench labs use batteries or isolated extra-low-voltage supplies. Mains workshop builds need verified isolation, stored-energy checks and a competent electrical team. Energised work needs a model-specific procedure and an equipped test station. Australian equipment repair and installation have state-specific licensing requirements. Low-voltage cabling and security work can also have registration requirements. T09 · Mains, earthing and safety.
