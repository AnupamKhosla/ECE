T54

Source resistance, loading and measurement uncertainty

Learn why voltage changes under load and how a meter, amplifier input or tolerance changes the result.

Time 20–35 minutesBefore this T02, T04, T24

Replace a linear source with a bench model

At a particular pair of terminals, a linear DC network behaves as an ideal voltage Vth in series with Rth. With a load RL, the measured output is VthRL/(Rth+RL). A 5 V source with 100 Ω in series reads 2.5 V into 100 Ω. This model applies around the tested condition. A current-limited supply changes behaviour when the requested current exceeds its setting. A battery or oxidised contact can also depart from a fixed resistance model.

See how a multimeter connects differently for voltage and current.

Understand what your reading includes

A 10 MΩ voltage input placed across a 1 MΩ load is a parallel resistance. A current-range meter introduces burden voltage in series. Resistance mode drives its own test current, and other paths on the board often change the result. Read data-sheet input resistance, accuracy format and range resolution. For an illustrative ±(0.5% of reading + 2 counts) meter on a 0.01 V resolution range at 5.00 V, the stated contribution is ±0.045 V before probes or contact effects.

Make uncertainty useful

Predict a range before measuring. For an ideal 5 V supply and nominally identical divider resistors with ±5% tolerance, opposite extremes predict 2.375–2.625 V. A reading of 2.47 V is plausible. A loaded midpoint of 1.67 V suggests a changed circuit. Take a second measurement that can distinguish these explanations.
