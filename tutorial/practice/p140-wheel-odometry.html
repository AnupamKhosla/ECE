P140

Calibrate wheel odometry against floor measurements

Drive an L-shaped route on paper. Fit wheel scale with a straight run and track width with a turn, then check both on a different route.

Time 55–80 minutesBefore this P42 · T59

Find two independent references

Count 2,400 decoded counts per wheel turn. In your recorded straight calibration, both wheels report 23,506 counts for 2.000 m of measured floor travel. The travel scale is approximately 0.0851 mm per count, corresponding to about 65.0 mm diameter. In a separate 360° counterclockwise spin, the left and right wheels report −5,538 and +5,538 counts. Estimate effective track width from the difference in wheel travel divided by 2π. Compare repeated clockwise and anticlockwise measurements to detect slip or asymmetry.

Replay an independent route

Start at (0, 0) facing +x. For each leg, convert signed counts to distance using circumference ÷ decoded counts per revolution. Integrate using T59. The floor survey of this synthetic validation run gives (1.000 m, 0.500 m) at about +90°. The replay changes the estimator’s scale or the input log while keeping that surveyed reference fixed.

  2. Try the reversed sign. Explain why equal physical wheel travel now becomes a modelled spin. For the spin-on-last-leg log, find the first segment where the estimate disagrees with the physical survey.
  3. If you have a small trainer, repeat the two calibration manoeuvres in a clear flat area, then validate on a distinct route. Record surface, payload, speed, wheel wear and errors at intermediate points. Stop the trainer before handling it.
Check the calibration and expected headings

Track width is about 150 mm. At 65 mm diameter and 150 mm track, the turn gives about 90.03°. At 165 mm it gives about 81.84°. A correct start and a heading error after the turn point to track width or slip. The location of the first disagreement helps distinguish them.
