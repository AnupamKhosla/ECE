Time a two-axis robot move

Give two joints the same endpoint and compare their progress with independent trapezoids, a shared trapezoid and a smooth shared profile. Calculate the motion time before adding a measured settling interval and tooling proof.

Compare endpoints, intermediate positions and the cycle

  1. Compare the default shared and independent ramps at t = 1 s. Both finish the full move at t = 2 s. Record why the joint 2 positions differ and how you would check tool clearance for each path.
  2. Keep endpoints and switch to the quintic. Record the limiting derivative and compare acceleration at the start and end. Then set joint 1 to 18° and joint 2 to −6° and compare the short moves. Which ramp shape and which quintic constraint apply?
  3. Suppose you measured 0.37 s from a shared ramp’s scheduled end until the axis held ±0.4° for 100 ms. Your simulated grip proof then takes 0.32 s. The station’s agreed deadline is 2.70 s from motion start to confirmed grip. Compute the margin and propose an evidence-based change. For quintic timing the observed settling interval is 0.12 s and grip proof still takes 0.32 s. Work both cases.
Check the timing and cycle decisions

At 1 s the shared profile reaches +45° and −15°. For independent ramps joint 2 has already stopped at −30°. For the 18° and −6° case, the shared ramp profile is triangular and takes about 0.775 s. The short quintic is jerk-limited at about 1.310 s. The long shared ramps reach grip proof at 2.69 s, only 10 ms before the deadline. The long quintic reaches it at about 3.25 s despite the faster settling. Review repeated timing, clamp proof and mechanical response in the configured payload range before changing the move.

Keep the chosen profile, axis limits, a speed and position trace, worst observed settling and the time of part proof. A successful move command and a confirmed grip are separate events in the station sequence.
