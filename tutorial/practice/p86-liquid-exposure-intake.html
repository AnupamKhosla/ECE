P86

Triage a phone or laptop after liquid exposure

Decide when to stop power, preserve customer data and hand the device to a trained repairer. Inspect a safe donor board on the bench.

Time 25–40 minutesBefore this P01 bench safety · T48 liquid corrosion

Separate a wet port warning from a spill inside

Record the liquid, location, time, whether the device was powered and what was done afterwards. A port warning can reflect moisture or debris at the connector. Liquid under a keyboard, inside a lens or around multiple boards has a wider scope. Heat, swelling, smoke, hissing or a damaged battery changes the handling decision immediately. Do not charge, power-cycle, heat, puncture or open a device with an active battery hazard. Keep people clear and follow local emergency or hazardous-device procedures. T48 · Leakage, corrosion and the limits of a dry surface.

For an ordinary exposure with a stable device, make the power source safe to disconnect, stop use and power the device off if safe to do so. Leave it unpowered and seek the model-specific service route when internal ingress is possible. Avoid forcing anything into a wet port. A device that switches on today can develop a delayed fault. Inspect a disconnected scrap board without adding damage

Use an already retired, dry module with no cell or external power. If you lack a donor, use the photos and sketch the likely affected paths. Pick a light, magnifier, screw tray and a camera. Handle a board only under its own appropriate ESD and service procedure. Keep powered evaluation, chemical cleaning and high-value data recovery within the scope of a trained technician.

  1. Divide the board photo into regions. Find the external opening, flex tails, sockets and the route liquid could have followed by gravity or capillary action. Label deposits, dull or missing pads, damaged traces and exposed connector contacts separately.

  2. Compare a known-good scrap connector of the same type. Photograph the differences before any cleaning. A discoloured liquid contact indicator can support an exposure history. Its condition does not tell you which net failed or when it happened.

  3. Mark the places you can inspect without lifting a shield or flex. Write what remains hidden. Make one scope decision about what you would assess, quote or refer. Leave customer authentication, encryption and data recovery to the authorised workflow.
