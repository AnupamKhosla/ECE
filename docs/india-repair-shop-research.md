The evidence gives examples of customers, service coverage and barriers. Private repair volumes, current turnaround, brand-level in-house capacity and willingness to pay still require direct checks. A tender is an example of buyer behaviour. Its closing date and qualification requirements determine whether it is actionable.

## Decide which service earns a test bench

1. Review a month of shop records and ask existing customers which portable machines or detached modules they can drop off. Start from repeat jobs passing through the counter. Contact parts suppliers for those exact models.
2. For each repeated model, ask three service channels about diagnosis fee, turnaround, in-house test capability, parts and warranty. Check one authorised provider, one local specialist and one dealer or courier route where those options exist.
3. Save model, complaint, destination, paid bill if shared, downtime and a forward contact who can authorise spend. Record unsuccessful and replacement-only cases too. Keep customer or supplier quotes private.
4. Begin with a single paid diagnostic on a supported part. Keep the test outcome, time, net contribution and repeat fault record. Compare against the current shop’s average contribution and bench time before stocking.

Useful next lessons will depend on the first repeat models. A repeat band-sealer model would support a mechanical alignment and documented wear-part replacement lesson. A refrigeration or compression partner would allow supervised examples with pressure, flow or temperature logs.
