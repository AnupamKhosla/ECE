P153

Close the speed loop on a small geared motor

Count a real encoder and measure motor speed before adding feedback. Compare the logged result with the browser model and prove that the bench controller stops when the feedback disappears.

Time Two bench sessionsBefore this P13 · P42 · P139

Assemble a single, secured drive

The example uses a 5 V Arduino Uno R3, a Pololu TB6612FNG carrier, the listed 6 V low-power gearmotor and a switched, current-limited extra-low-voltage motor supply. Secure the motor with its output clear of cables and fingers. Use an unloaded shaft with a small visible mark. A reachable motor supply switch lets you remove motor power independently of the sketch. Use P139 if the hardware is unavailable.

Predict what the serial log will show

The encoder supplies 12 decoded edges per motor revolution when counting both edges on both channels. The sketch counts just rising edges of channel A, giving 3 per motor revolution. Multiply by 100.37 motor revolutions per gearbox output turn for about 301.1 observed edges per output revolution. In a 100 ms window 30 edges gives about 59.8 rpm. One edge changes the result by about 2 rpm. A single channel measures speed magnitude. When you need signed motion, decode both channels as in P42.

Download the Uno training sketch. Save it in a folder named wheel-speed-trainer, then open it in the Arduino IDE. The encoder interrupt counts edges and timestamps the most recent edge. The main loop reads both together, divides the count change by the actual elapsed sample time and limits duty to 60%. PI holds the previous duty when the last edge is older than 30 ms or the sample window has no edges. The missing-feedback timeout can still disable the bridge. Prove stop, speed and feedback in order

  1. Upload with the motor supply switched off. At 115200 baud, confirm that holding the run switch gives a no_encoder_edges message after the grace period. Keep holding the switch and verify there is no new run. Release it before attempting another run. Keep this missing-feedback trial unpowered at VMOT.
  2. With run released, switch on the motor supply. In open-loop mode, hold run briefly at the lower target. Record target, measured speed, duty and edge count. Release and confirm drive is disabled. Measure the same speed independently by marking the shaft and counting turns over several seconds or using recorded video.
  3. Repeat with the higher target and compare the speed change. Power down the motor supply and re-upload with closedLoop set to true. Compare the same two commands, returning to idle between runs. Use T31 to explain why duty may settle at a different value.
  4. Keep one open-loop and one PI trace and name one limitation of each. A missing-edge timeout detects absent counts. It cannot prove direction or reveal motion between an encoder and gearbox output when a coupling slips. This bench sketch uses an unloaded small motor. Its inputs are ordinary training controls.

If the accepted count disagrees with the encoder specification after a marked turn, measure the edge at the receiver as in P154 · Encoder signal integrity. Use P173 · Execute both compiled Uno builds with emulated pins to repeat load changes and missing feedback without hardware.
