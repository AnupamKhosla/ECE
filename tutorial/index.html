ECE Workbench

Build a foundation in electronics by working at the bench. Then use what you can measure and explain to repair equipment, commission building controls or build your own systems.

Start here

Open P00 · Your ECE foundation and learning routes for your first three bench sessions and four skill checkpoints. Begin with P01 · Set up your bench and P02 · Build your first circuit. Keep T00 · Maths toolkit nearby for units and formulas.

After the foundation
  • Welding-set repair starts with the shop learning plan and a battery-powered rectifier lab.
  • Building controls and site work starts by finding, mounting and labelling a room sensor. Continue with BMS fundamentals, a damper case, 4–20 mA input calibration and a BACnet point check.
  • Industrial automation and service starts with a PLC start/stop trainer. Continue with SCADA trend diagnosis, VFD condition checks and pneumatic actuators.
  • Laptop repair starts with the parts of a desktop PC, then shows how to identify and service a laptop’s SSD and RAM.
  • Android phone repair teaches screen, loudspeaker and headphone jack service on a Pixel 4a. Continue with Pixel 4a battery replacement or Galaxy A54 USB-C diagnosis.
  • Raspberry Pi and Linux starts with setting up the board. Then wire an LED and log temperature with Python before using the Pi to collect readings from an ESP32.
  • Embedded controllers and IoT covers stop logic, timers, Pico hardware debugging and telemetry that flags stale values.
  • Robotics starts with a line follower. Diagnose its motor and encoder, then try arm positioning and ROS 2 simulation.
  • Machine vision starts with a camera and controlled lighting. Continue to 3D depth, AI inspection on unseen parts and a simulated robot cell.
  • Electronics design and test covers KiCad, a board test fixture, CAN bus, sensor calibration and power logging.
  • Antennas, RF and fibre starts with an indoor antenna receive test. Continue with RF reflection or fibre loss.
  • Choose a job route using the Australian job-ad research and employer examples. Keep a portfolio record for the work you can demonstrate.

How to use the two books

Build in the Practice Book and follow its Why it works links to the Theory Book when a part or reading needs an explanation. Find a part by name or symbol in the part index, or find a source course in the practical learning library.

Bench practice and real equipment

Construction labs use batteries or isolated extra-low-voltage supplies. The welding track also explains hazardous-voltage equipment: inside-machine work needs verified isolation, stored-energy checks and a competent repair mentor; energised diagnosis needs the model-specific service procedure and an equipped technician. In Australia, installation work and equipment repair have state-specific licensing requirements; T09 explains the distinction. ELV work can also require cabling or security registration.
