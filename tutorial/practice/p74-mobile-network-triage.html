P74

Triage a mobile coverage or activation fault

Use an Android phone to compare cellular measurements and service behaviour across locations. Write a useful carrier fault report.

Phone and notebook40–60 minAfter P31

Know the equipment

A handset connects over the air to a cell. Site radios and antennas serve areas called sectors. A backhaul link takes data from the site towards the operator's network. In some sites the radio and antenna are separate. An active antenna system can combine them. Carrier support may investigate registration, the SIM or eSIM account, voice compatibility and outages. A field team may investigate the site or radio path. Read T44 on the mobile service path and signal readings.

See how a walk-test kit works. Compare the equipment used to log both LTE and 5G measurements with what you can record from your Android phone. Rohde & Schwarz on YouTube.

Make a repeatable comparison

  1. Use your own Android phone and service. Switch off Wi-Fi for the comparison so mobile data and ordinary test calls use the intended cellular connection. Note the handset model, SIM slot if relevant, carrier, time and displayed radio technology. Android Settings often supplies SIM status or signal strength. The path and available measurements vary by manufacturer.
  2. At a stationary indoor point, take three readings about 15 seconds apart. Record radio technology, signal name and units, plus whether an ordinary data request and a call to a consenting person complete. Use LTE RSRP or NR SS-RSRP when clearly labelled. Write “unavailable” for anything your handset hides. Never call an emergency number to test a device.
  3. Repeat near a window and at a safe outdoor point on the ground. Keep the same device, SIM and test actions. If it changes radio technology, record the change. Compare LTE with LTE and NR with NR where available.
  4. Where practical, have a second consenting person compare their phone on the same carrier at the same time. A known-working compatible phone or a permitted test SIM helps separate handset and subscription faults. Keep identifiers and account information within the provider's normal verification process.
Example observationReasonable next action
One indoor location has weak LTE RSRP. Outdoor signal and calls improve on the same device.Record the location dependence. Check provider coverage guidance and supported Wi-Fi calling.
Two same-carrier devices stop working in the same area. Both work elsewhere.Check the public outage notice, then report the timing and area using the provider's channel.
One handset fails to attach everywhere. Another compatible handset on the same service works at the test point.Check SIM activation or eSIM status, model compatibility and recent device changes with the provider. If only voice fails while data works, ask about VoLTE provisioning and device support.
Signal is stronger outdoors but service still fails at some times.Record time, signal quality if available and the exact failing service. Consider congestion, outage, backhaul and provisioning among the possible layers.

The lab uses an ordinary handset from public or private ground-level locations. Site access, climbing, RF work and repeaters require the relevant authority and procedures. Consult ACMA's repeater rules and Australian handset compatibility guidance.

Submit your evidence

Keep three rows of measurements, units and service results, plus an incident note that distinguishes the symptom, tested conditions and the next check. Omit phone numbers, SIM identifiers and precise private locations.
