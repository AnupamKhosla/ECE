Build one circuit at a time

Before buying parts, check the short shopping list and meter exercise. Once you can read an AA cell, follow what happens inside the meter. The bench-to-mains route connects the same measurements to isolated AC and local electrical training.

  1. Start with a battery, a resistor and an LED. Check which breadboard holes share a clip and measure the current from the resistor voltage. P182 · First LED and resistor exercise · T01 · Current, voltage and a closed loop.
  2. Turn a diode around, then build a single-diode rectifier and a four-diode bridge. Add a capacitor and time how the voltage falls after power goes off. P182 · Diode, rectifier and capacitor exercises · T11 · Diode theory.
  3. Switch that LED with a transistor. Combine it with the bridge and capacitor, then use the meter to trace the combined circuit stage by stage. P182 · Transistor switch and combined build · T12 · BJT theory.
  4. Find the same three LED connections on a solderable board and repeat your measurements. Put the circuit into KiCad when you can explain the copper path. T26 · Copper, laminate and pads · P48 · Draw and lay out a simple PCB.

Use the stages below for more practice and to choose what to build next. P00 · Bench setup and learning routes · T00 · Maths for the measurements.

Look for the rows of contacts and the power rails when the underside of the breadboard is open. Moritz Klein · Beginner's guide to breadboards.
