P51

Calibrate a load cell and test for drift

A scale reads zero with no load and shows the wrong weight in the middle of the range. Prove the fixture, bridge wiring and calibration separately.

Time 55–80 minutesBefore this P12 · P24 · P37
What you’ll use

A small beam load cell with a manual and rated capacity suited to the exercise, an HX711 breakout with a documented supply/interface voltage, a compatible microcontroller, and safe reference masses. The sample readings below work without hardware.

Identify the supported end and the loaded end

A strain-gauge bridge turns stretching and compression into a small differential voltage. The amplifier and analogue-to-digital converter resolve the voltage and yield counts. A rigid mounting surface and the correct force direction matter as much as the wiring.

Use the manufacturer’s E+/E− and signal+/signal− assignments. Colours can differ. On the breakout, confirm which terminals expect the bridge signal and which digital pins connect to your MCU. SparkFun HX711 photos and hookup guide.

Fit two points and independently check the middle

  1. Mount the cell as instructed and secure the fixture so a loose weight cannot fall. Sample zero after warm-up. Use the average of several steady readings. Record the spread.
  2. Place a known 1.000 kg reference on a cell and fixture rated for the load. Record the stable count. Fit the slope from zero and this mass. Keep calibration masses within the device’s rated working region.
  3. Check with a different reference mass at 0.500 kg. Approach it once from below and once from above. Remove the load and check whether zero returns.

Tell drift from noise and mechanical binding

Hold the load for one minute, sampling the average and spread in the first and final ten seconds. Counts moving slowly with no movement suggests drift or creep. Counts jumping randomly suggest noise, poor contacts or movement. The same mass reading differently from above and below can point to fixture friction, mechanical hysteresis or setup asymmetry. Swapping wires changes sign or corrupts the signal, so use the pinout to rule it out.

Keep raw and scaled values

Save counts, calibration constants, mass, error percentage or grams, direction and settling time. Define one limit before declaring pass. For this dataset, a 5 g verification limit catches the falling middle-point error and prompts another fixture check.
