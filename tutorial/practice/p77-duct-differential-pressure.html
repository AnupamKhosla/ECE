P77

Find a fault in a duct pressure measurement

Trace the two air tubes, make a water manometer, and separate a bad pressure reading from a dirty filter.

Time 35–50 minutesBefore this P03 meter · P37 current loop

For a terminal box that uses differential pressure to estimate its airflow, P88 compares the VAV reading with an independent reference at two setpoints.

Find the filter, the two taps and the signal

In an air handler, find the filter on the drawing before following its tubes. One tap measures upstream static pressure and the other measures downstream static pressure. For a filter with flow in the intended direction, the upstream side is usually higher. A differential-pressure gauge shows the difference locally. A transmitter sends a number to the controller. A pressure switch provides an on/off proof or alarm at a set difference. Identify the range, units, fittings and output type before comparing them. T45 · Pressure, range and proof.

Watch DwyerOmega’s Magnehelic demonstration. Find the two pressure connections and the units on the gauge. Compare the dial with the electronic transmitter's output in the 605 datasheet. DwyerOmega’s Magnehelic demonstration on YouTube.

Build a water-column reference

  1. Use about 1 m of clear flexible tube, a ruler, a board, clips or ties, coloured water and a small clean syringe. Secure the tubing in a U on the board with both ends up. Add enough water to leave a visible level in each leg and generous air space above the water. Keep this rig on the bench, away from electronics.

  2. With both ends open, the heights should be equal. Read the two surfaces at eye level. Connect the empty syringe directly to the air space at one top end, leaving the other leg open. Move the plunger a little and stop at a stable height difference of 10–20 mm. Keep water away from the syringe and do not seal the second leg.

  3. Measure the vertical difference between the two water levels, even though each one moved. For room-temperature water, 10 mm is about 98 Pa and 20 mm about 196 Pa. The lower liquid surface is on the higher-pressure side. Vent the first leg and verify the zero returns.

Keep the liquid rig disconnected from an electronic transmitter unless its manufacturer specifies a compatible dry test setup. To practise the electrical side, use the simulator in P37. On a unidirectional 0–250 Pa transmitter with a 4–20 mA linear output, a known 100 Pa should give 10.4 mA. A 250 Ω shunt would show 2.60 V. Measure the output and the controller input separately in a real low-voltage trainer.
