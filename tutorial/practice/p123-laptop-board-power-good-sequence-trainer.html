P123

Find a missing enable or power-good signal before swapping a board

Model standby, an enable and a power-good signal at safe voltage, then compare the first failed transition on a documented donor board.

Time 55–85 minutesBefore this P33, P97, P105

Read a chain in order

Draw input supply, standby rail, a controller request, a switched rail and its power-good feedback. A real board may have several coupled rails, active-low signals, mask-dependent timing and pull-ups fed by other rails. Use an authorised diagram and the exact board revision, including thresholds, polarity and probe test points. The bench exercise uses an independent low-voltage trainer. It illustrates the logic behind the measurements in T27 · Board power rails, enables and startup sequencing.

Tektronix prepares a passive scope probe for reliable timing measurements.

Make and break an observable signal chain

Use an isolated 5 V supply limited to 30 mA, a two-position SPDT switch, a documented SN74HC14 DIP, two 10 kΩ resistors and a 100 nF bypass. Supply the logic from the standby rail. Tie unused inputs to return and leave outputs open. Wire the switch common to EN node, one throw to 5 V and the other to return. Fit 10 kΩ from EN to return and use a removable jumper from EN to the first inverter’s input. Fit another 10 kΩ from the input to return so the input stays defined with the jumper removed. Use the first inverter output as the trainer’s active-low status proxy.

  1. With the switch off expect standby near 5 V, EN near 0 V and active-low status near 5 V. With the switch on expect EN near 5 V and status near 0 V. This status is a logic model of feedback. No regulated output is built in this exercise.
  2. Power off and remove the EN jumper. Then power on and switch again. Standby remains present and EN moves but the inverter input stays low. Trace from the last node that changes to the first node that does not, and restore the jumper with power off.
  3. On a documented spare board, compare power-button event, expected enable and power-good in order under the approved normal power condition. Use only the specified ground and designated pads. Scope EN and status on a common timebase if their signal limits and grounding permit it. If the enable is absent, investigate upstream permissions. If enable is present but power-good is missing, verify that rail’s voltage, load and feedback path. Keep the annotated timeline before choosing a part.
